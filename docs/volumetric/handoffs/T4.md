# T4 hand-off

Status: done

## What I built

The volumetric core: the primary march, the density model and the lighting, in the three GLSL modules T4 owns. Lab
state A now shows a cumulus field with sunlit tops, flat darker bases and silver-lined edges, shrinking to the horizon.
Until M1 merges T1 and T2, the committed shader runs on a temporary in-shader noise (`VOL_TEMP_NOISE`).

- **March** (`marchClouds`):
  - It finds the ray's span in the spherical shell for a camera below the base, inside the layer or above the top,
    and returns nothing when the ground is in the way.
  - The marched segment is clamped to `VOL_MAX_MARCH` (60 km) from the shell entry.
  - Steps are spaced geometrically (each step is a fixed fraction of its own distance), so near-horizon rays get
    long steps and overhead rays short ones.
  - The start is jittered with blue noise. The march exits early at transmittance < 0.01.
  - Integration uses Hillaire's energy-conserving form, `col += T · L · (1 − e^(−σ·dt))`.
  - Steps past 25 to 90 km fade to shape-only density (the lod).
  - `gCloudDist` is the contribution-weighted mean distance. The result goes through `applyAerial`.
  - The five `?debug=` views: weather, density (optical depth), transmittance, light (mean luminance) and depth
    (`gCloudDist`, normalised to 120 km).
- **Density** (`cloudDensity`, `heightFraction`, `weatherAt`), after Schneider 2015 and 2017:
  - SHAPE R is remapped by its GBA fbm, then multiplied by the type's height gradient. The type is read per texel
    from the map's G channel:
    - stratus is a thin, flat layer;
    - cumulus has a flat base and a rounded top;
    - cumulonimbus is a column up to `CB_TOP`.
  - A coverage remap follows. A cumulonimbus raises its coverage toward 1 above 62–85% of its height, which spreads
    the anvil.
  - DETAIL erosion is wispy at the base and billowy at the top. It is gentler for stratus, so the deck stays
    continuous.
  - The field moves with the weather offset, the tops lean downwind (shear), and the detail drifts with `uTime`.
  - Rain clouds (map B) are denser.
- **Lighting** (`cloudPhase`, `cloudRadiance`, `applyAerial`), after Schneider and Hillaire 2016:
  - A light march of `VOL_LIGHT_STEPS` (6) steps toward the light: 70 m first, then ×1.75 each step, about 3.3 km
    in all. The first two steps include detail.
  - Beer–Lambert through 3 multiple-scattering octaves (a 0.5, b 0.45, c 0.6), with the dual-lobe HG
    (0.8 / −0.2, mixed 0.5).
  - The powder term is faded out when looking toward the light.
  - A two-stream diffuse-transmission term (see Decisions).
  - Ambient goes from horizon colour on the bases to zenith (+ a little mid) colour on the tops, by height fraction.
  - The key light is the sun by day and the moon at night (`uMoonFraction`), and the sun colour follows elevation
    (`uSunColor`).
  - Precipitation darkens the base.
  - Aerial perspective fills the cloud's covered fraction with `skyGradient(rd)`, by `1 − e^(−dist / 55 km)`.

## Decisions (for M1 and T8 to carry into DECISIONS.md)

1. **`MAX_MARCH` is the length of the marched segment, measured from the shell entry.** It is not an absolute
   distance from the camera.
   - At the horizon the base is ~138 km away. An absolute 60 km cap would leave a cloudless band about 1.2° tall
     (~17 px) above the horizon.
   - With the cap on the segment, clouds reach the horizon and the haze takes them.
2. **Steps are spaced geometrically** between entry and exit (`t = t0 · (t1/t0)^x`, `dt ∝ t`), so a step's
   screen-space footprint is roughly constant. When the camera is inside the shell, `t0` is held at ≥ 0.2 km.
3. **A two-stream diffuse transmission term was added**, beyond the brief's Beer–Lambert + powder + HG:
   - The formula is `T_d ≈ 1 / (1 + ¾·τ·(1 − g))`. Only the part above `e^(−τ)` is added, weighted 0.35.
   - The reason: Beer–Lambert alone, even with the octaves, made backlit cumulus bodies slate-black and the
     overcast deck near-black. Real thick clouds lose light as roughly 1/τ, not exponentially.
   - A weight of 1.0 washed out the bases, so 0.35 is a balance. T8 may retune it.
4. **The light path's extinction is 0.55 × the view path's** (`LIGHTING_ABSORB`), standing in for light that
   multiple scattering carries deeper.
5. **The type is read per texel from the map's G channel** (T2's decision 2). Only the shell's outer radius uses the
   global `uCloudType`, so a non-cumulonimbus sky doesn't march to 10 km.
6. **Erosion depends on the type**: 0.22 for stratus and 0.6 for cumulus and cumulonimbus. Cumulus-strength erosion
   punched holes in the 0.95 overcast deck.
7. **Aerial perspective leaves transmittance alone.** It replaces the cloud's colour with the sky's, so a distant
   cloud still hides the sun (needed for T5's `cloudAtSun`).
8. **One key light.** The light march runs toward the sun, or toward the moon when the sun is down, never both, to
   halve the cost.
9. **Scales:**

   | Constant | Value | Means |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | SHAPE tile | 10 km | T1's lowest Worley cell is about 2.5 km |
   | DETAIL tile | 0.6 km | features of about 300, 150 and 75 m |
   | extinction | 80 per km at density 1 | |

10. **Sources:** the papers in BRIEF §2 only, and no code was ported. The temporary noise's hash is the folklore
    `fract(sin(dot(…)) · 43758.5453)`, and it leaves with `VOL_TEMP_NOISE`.

## Known issues

- **Per-frame grain.** The blue-noise jitter changes every frame and nothing accumulates it yet, so edges and
  distant clouds speckle in a single frame. T7's temporal reprojection is what removes it.
- **Cost.**
  - The full-resolution single pass costs 6–11 ms with the real textures. T7's 0.35× target and checkerboard
    should get it under budget.
  - The temporary noise costs 32–95 ms. That is fine for the lab until M1.
- **Dusk (B):** the shadowed sides are a little brown-orange, because the ambient on the bases is the horizon
  colour, which is orange at dusk. Left for T8, since the brief specifies the horizon colour for the bases.
- **Night:** moonlit clouds come out somewhat brighter than the night sky. This needs a look in T8, after T5's
  stars and moon land.
- **Overcast (C):** the far horizon under the deck shows the blue-white haze from `skyGradient`. It is plausible,
  but T8 should compare it against photos.
- **No in-cloud camera test.** The camera is always 2 m up, so the "inside / above the shell" branches of
  `marchSpan` have not been exercised visually.
