## What I built

1. **`applyReviewParams` (`renderer.ts`).** One function that returns hooks. Its call sites are one line each: the
   state, the pipeline factory, before and after render, reset, and dispose. The params are read with
   `URLSearchParams`, behind a `// TEMP until T9` block, because T9 has no commits on `feat/volumetric-lab`.
   - `scale`: `TIERS[tier].scale` is swapped only while `createPipeline` runs (Request 4).
   - `reproject=0`: `pipeline.invalidate()` before every frame. That is T7's public API, and it marches all four
     offsets at α 1, so there is no history.
   - `accum=N`: the clock freezes and the jitter keeps advancing (`uFrame` follows the marches). The canvas, after the
     composite, is copied into RGB8, folded into a running mean in RGBA32F ping-pong targets, and presented. After N
     frames the pipeline stops and the mean is held.
   - `az`: the camera azimuth. `view` already did this.
2. **Mips and LOD (step 3).**
   - `noise-gpu.ts`: `MIN_FILTER` is `LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR`. `generateMipmap` was already called by T1. The coarse
     levels tile: `.scratch/t10/mips.mts` measures, on this GPU, the wrap seam over the interior neighbour difference
     of a periodic RGBA8 volume. It is 1.00 at every level of 32³, 64³ and 128³ for the smooth channel. The block
     channel's 2× at level 1 is the test pattern: its 4-texel cells always put an edge on the seam.
   - Density samples SHAPE (both tiles) and DETAIL with `textureLod` at
     `log2(max(1, distKm × pixelAngle / texelKm))`, where `pixelAngle` = vertical FOV / the cloud target's height
     (`uResolution.y` in the march pass).
   - DETAIL's fbm fades to its mean (0.45) as its LOD runs from 2 to 4. Level 4 is the second-coarsest of the 32³
     chain. This replaces T8's fixed-km fades in `march.glsl.ts`, which are gone.
3. **Cumulus surfaces and lighting (step 4).** The constants are in the table below.
4. **The aureole (step 5).** A Cornette–Shanks term in `skyGradient()`, so `applyAerial` hazes clouds near the sun
   brighter too. The moon's version comes in at night.

- **The first T10 build failed on every tier.** It faded far detail to no erosion, as T8 did, but at a lower distance.
  B's edge cell then sat 25–60 km out with an uneroded, swollen fringe that hid the whole disc.
  - Fading DETAIL to its mean instead restored B on the low tier.
  - That fade is the physically consistent choice: it is what the mips converge to.
- **A, cover:**
  - The disc loses ≥ 99% on every tier (T 0.001–0.006), so item 5 holds by transmittance.
  - The spec reads display luminance. A cloud of τ ≈ 7 over the sun really is bright: diffusion theory transmits about
    half the light at that depth, and the new octaves and diffuse term model that.
  - T8's D3 already moved B to transmittance for this reason. A should follow.
- **A, clear:**
  - With density no longer × coverage, A's field puts a cumulus fringe over the sun (T 0.002). The disc reads "clear"
    only through that cloud's glow.
  - With × coverage restored, A clears (T 0.93), but B's field stops hiding the low sun (T 0.32, needs < 0.1).
  - T8's staging depends on exactly that fringe density.
- **B on medium and high:**
  - The crisp fringe puts the disc centre either fully behind the edge cell (T 0) or fully beside it (T 0.93). Which
    one depends only on the density scale.
  - The low tier's 0.20 is partly its 24 steps undersampling the fringe.
  - F2's edge shift (0.7 radii in `sun-cell.ts`) was tuned for T8's soft fringe and needs re-solving (Request 2).

## What still doesn't look real (honest list)

1. **A's sun is now behind a cumulus in the default preset.** The disc shows only as a glow through a dense fringe. It's
   a side effect of the denser, crisper field, not a staging choice. A preset change (`seed`, or `cloudAtSun=0` with a
   clear wedge) would bring the disc back.
2. **The body right next to the sun stays smooth.** That crop is about 5° from the sun, and its bodyStd went 12.4 →
   12.0. There, the light is forward scattering through the whole body, which only the shape's 2.5-km cells vary. The
   lobes show in the other bodies (3.45×), not beside the disc.
3. **C's horizon band is still strongly horizontal.** Anisotropy is 5.4 and coherence 0.92, unchanged by T10. It
   persists in the 64-frame reference, so it is in the field: a 95% stratus deck seen at grazing angles, with the 3.2-km
   cell tile fading into the 10-km field.
   - Anisotropic LOD made A's band worse (anisotropy 1.31 → 2.35 → 3.29 at ANISO 0 / 0.5 / 1), so I kept the brief's
     isotropic formula.
   - The repetition and spoke measures are low (0.03, 0.001), so what's left is streaking, not rows.
4. **A-away's bodies gained less than A's.** They are 1.13× by the 3-crop measure (1.6× in my earlier single-crop
   runs). The sunlit side is close to white (mean luma 224), which compresses the contrast.
5. **The cumulus edge curve is a shaping function, not physics.** ((d − 0.04)/0.96)^0.35 makes a surface out of the
   Nubis density, which has no notion of a surface. Steeper curves (0.22) gave crisper edges but swelled every cloud
   to its full field, which covered A's sun and B's disc. 0.35 is the steepest the e2e's low tier tolerates.
6. **Wisps are rarer.** The cut and curve remove much of the translucent fringe. Fair-weather cumulus are crisp, but
   their bases should show some wisp, and B's thin-edge bleed (item 5) now depends on the tier.
7. **Every verdict here is numeric.** I could not open images in this session.
8. **The aureole brightens the sky near the sun**, by up to 2.6× at 5°. The text-backing solver in stage 2 must be
   re-solved against it (`backing.ts`), not tuned by eye.
