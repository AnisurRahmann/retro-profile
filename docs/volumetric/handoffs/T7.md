# T7 hand-off
Status: partial. Everything is built, and the mobile target is met (low 1.24–1.43 ms against ≤ 3). The desktop target
is not: high is 4.3–4.5 ms against ≤ 2.5, about 13× faster than before. The rest of the gap is the full-resolution
composite's fixed cost at 3840×2160 (see "Why desktop high is still OVER"). Closing it needs a decision from the
owner.

## What I built

- **Cloud target.** RGBA16F at `TIERS[tier].scale` × the drawing buffer, ping-ponged.
- **Checkerboard reprojection.**
  - Each march covers one pixel of every 2×2 block, in Bayer order (0,0), (1,1), (0,1), (1,0). It writes a half-size
    RGBA32F target, so the pass marches a quarter of the pixels.
  - The resolve reprojects the previous target with the wind. A world point P was at P − v·dt, where v is the
    weather map's velocity × `WEATHER_MAP_KM`, plus density's detail drift. The camera is static.
  - Depth comes from each fresh sample's `gCloudDist`. A clear ray falls back to the 3×3 maximum, then to the shell.
  - The history is clamped to the 3×3 box of fresh samples (no ghosting). A fresh pixel blends at α 0.25. History more
    than 0.2 outside the box is rejected, and the fresh sample replaces it.
- **Re-march ≤ 15 Hz.** `createMarchClock` runs on the real clock with 4 ms of slack, so 60 Hz marches every 4th frame.
  The mean never exceeds 15 Hz, and a stall doesn't cause a burst.
- **Composite every frame** at full resolution, with `displayColor(cloud.rgb + cloud.a × background)`:
  - **Upsample.** One bilinear tap first. Where the transmittance has an edge (its quad derivative is ≥ 0.03 per pixel),
    it takes the 4-tap bilateral: bilinear × exp(−4·|T_tap − T_bilinear|). Clear sky reads the display cache directly.
  - **Wind shift.** The clouds are shifted by the wind since the last resolve, through a 64×36 velocity grid, so the
    drift is smooth at 60 Hz between 15 Hz marches.
- **Background caches.** `background()` costs about 3.7 ms at 3840×2160, so it isn't evaluated per frame:
  - Two full-resolution caches: linear RGBA16F, and RGBA8 after `displayColor`.
  - Both are redrawn on every 4th march (3.75 Hz), so the stars still twinkle and the sun still breathes.
- **Resets.** The first frame, a resize and every `setState` (through `invalidate()`) start a reset:
  - The frame marches all four offsets and fills both caches.
  - α then runs 1, ½, ⅓ per pixel sample before settling at 0.25 (`historyAlpha`).
- **Tiers.** `auto` starts high on a fine pointer and medium on a coarse one. When the median rAF interval stays over
  20 ms for 3 s, it steps down one tier and watches a fresh window. The log reads `[vol] auto → medium: median 20+ ms / 3 s`.
- **GPU timing (renderer.ts).** `window.__vol.gpuMs` is now the mean of the last 60 measured frames, each finished query
  counted once. Up to 8 queries are in flight, so every frame is measured. See Decisions D6.

### Why desktop high is still OVER

All measured at A high, 3840×2160, with the march disabled where needed:

| Cost | ms |
| --- | --- |
| Full-screen pass writing a constant (the floor for any full-res composite) | 0.83 |
| Copying a full-res RGBA8 cache to the canvas | 1.2–1.6 |
| The composite as built (clouds, caches, displayColor) | ~3.4 |
| Live `background()` + `displayColor` instead of the caches | 4.5 |
| March + resolve, amortised at 15 Hz | ~0.8–1.3 |
| Cache refresh at 3.75 Hz | ~0.2 |

- On this backend, every extra render pass costs roughly 0.2–0.6 ms, whatever its size. So frames without a march draw
  a single pass.
- Desktop medium and low are barely cheaper than high: the fixed full-resolution cost dominates, not the march.
- The same composite at 375×812 DPR 3 (2.7 M pixels) is about 1 ms. That's why mobile passes easily.

Options to close the gap. Each one trades against the brief, so the owner decides:

1. **Composite only on march frames when nothing else changed** (draw at 15 Hz and let the canvas hold): about 2.0 ms.
   The cost is that the drift steps at 15 Hz, about 1–2 px per step at typical elevations.
2. **Cap the lab's drawing buffer at DPR 1.5:** about 2.5 ms. Stars and the sun's limb get softer. This also needs a
   renderer change (T0), and it bends the target's own definition.
3. **Re-baseline the desktop target** at about 4.5 ms for 3840×2160. The full-res pass floor alone is a third of the
   current 2.5.
4. **Keep optimising** inside the spec: mediump math in the composite, fewer edge pixels, a cheaper `background()`
   (T5's dither runs 6 `pow`s per pixel). I'd expect about 0.5–1 ms, not 2.

## Decisions (for M1/T8 to carry into docs/DECISIONS.md)

- **D1: the march target is RGBA32F, with the distance packed into the alpha:** `floor(d × 32) + T`, with T clamped
  to 0.9998.
  - The header declares a single `out vec4 outColor` without a location, so MRT isn't possible.
  - fp32 keeps a 1/32 km distance and an 11-bit transmittance.
  - The cloud target itself is RGBA16F, as the brief says.
- **D2: the background is cached at full resolution and refreshed every 4th march (3.75 Hz).** Live, `background()` is
  ~3.7 ms per frame at 4K.
  - Banded refreshes with scissor or layers cost more: each pass has a fixed cost, and a scissored pass still loads and
    stores the whole target.
  - Every pass clears before drawing, so a tile-based GPU never loads the old contents.
- **D3: the bilateral upsample runs only where the transmittance has an edge** (quad derivative ≥ 0.03 per pixel).
  Elsewhere bilateral equals bilinear, and the edge-only path saves ~0.5 ms.
- **D4: the reprojection velocity includes density's `DENSITY_DETAIL_DRIFT`** (the constant is visible: density comes
  before reproject in the assembly).
  - Without it, detail moves ~20% faster than the history.
  - The 2×2 cadence turns that lag into stripes across the wind on thin cloud. Before/after crops:
    `.scratch/t7/zs5.jpg` (scratch, not committed).
- **D5: `uFrame` advances once per 4-march cycle.** `blueNoise` is indexed by the march target's pixel, so the four
  pixels of a block share a base value.
  - A per-march uFrame biases the four pixels against each other.
  - With one step per cycle, each pixel sees the clean golden-ratio sequence once per update.
- **D6: `window.__vol.gpuMs` is the mean of the last 60 measured frames.**
  - The pipeline's cost is periodic (a march every ~4–5 frames).
  - `lab:gpu` polls a single frame's value every 100 ms. That aliases with the 66.7 ms march period: a cell caught march
    frames either half the time or never, so `low` read above `high`.
  - `FrameTime.gpuMs` (and `times`) keep the per-frame value.
- **D7: the governor reads the rAF interval, not GPU time.**
  - The GPU time per frame is bimodal (most frames only composite), so its median would never see a slow march.
  - iOS has no timer query.
  - A 30 Hz-capped display steps down to low. That's acceptable.
- **D8: `invalidate()` resets the history on every `setState`.** A new state never ghosts, but each `setState` costs
  four marches (≈ 5–8 ms on desktop high), so a slider drag (T3) pays that per input event.

## Known issues

- **Desktop high is over its target** (above).
- **Softer than the full-res march.** At the 0.35 scale, edges are smoother than M1's per-pixel march, which had jitter
  grain instead. T8 judges checklist item 2. The scale is `TIERS` (T0). The bilateral sharpness and edge threshold are
  `REPROJECT_*`.
- **Thin sub-pixel wisps can be clamped away.** A feature only one non-fresh pixel sees is clamped to its fresh
  neighbours. That's the standard trade-off against ghosting.
- **The background animates at 3.75 Hz.** At night, twinkle frequencies above ~1.9 Hz alias to slower ones.
- **The first ~0.5 s after a reset** still shows some single-sample noise on thin cloud, while α converges.
- **The timer on ANGLE Metal.** Apple GPU clocks scale with load, so removing work doesn't lower the measured time
  proportionally. Each render pass also shows a fixed cost. Compare relative numbers between runs of the same day.
- **`gpuMs` > `rafMs` (M1's note)** no longer shows. With the 60-frame mean and 8 queries in flight, every frame is
  measured once.
