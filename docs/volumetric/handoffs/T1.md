# T1 hand-off

Status: done

## What I built

`createNoiseTextures(gl, tier)` generates the lab's three noise textures (BRIEF §5 "Noise", `NoiseTextures` in
`types.ts`), and `?debug=noise` shows them.

- **SHAPE**: 64³ RGBA8 (128³ on the high tier), rendered slice by slice on the GPU. R is Perlin-Worley: a Perlin fbm
  (octaves 4, 8, 16, and 32 at 128³) remapped by the inverted Worley at 4. G, B, A are inverted Worley fbm from 2, 4
  and 8 cells per tile (×1, ×2, ×4 octaves at ⅝, ¼, ⅛).
- **DETAIL**: 32³ RGBA8, inverted Worley fbm from 2, 4 and 8 (the highest frequency 32³ resolves at 4 texels a cell).
- **BLUE**: 64×64 R8, void-and-cluster blue noise on a torus, made on the CPU once per session.
- Every channel tiles in x, y and z, and spans 0..1.
- **`?debug=noise`**: eight square tiles (SHAPE R G B A, DETAIL R G B, BLUE) at a z picked on a slider, registered
  through `registerDebugView` from `debug-views.ts`. `renderer.ts` is untouched.

**Verified on the GPU** (Apple M2, Chrome's ANGLE Metal, headless Chromium driven by Playwright). The GPU's texels were
read back and compared with the CPU reference:

| | Values compared | Largest difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SHAPE 128³ | 286,208, sampled through the volume (every 5th slice, 3rd row, 2nd column) | 0.5 / 255 |
| SHAPE 64³ | 49,152 in the lab, 36,608 in isolation | 0.5 / 255 |
| DETAIL 32³ | every texel in the lab, a sample in isolation | 0.5 / 255 |

| SHAPE mip level 1 | layer 0 against the 2×2×2 mean of level 0 | 0.5 / 255 |

0.5/255 is the 8-bit rounding and nothing more: no value is further off. No GL errors, the framebuffer is complete.

## How to verify (commands, URLs, shot paths)

- `pnpm vitest run tests/unit/vol-noise.test.ts`: 59 tests in about 1 s.
  - Tileability: opposite faces of SHAPE 64³, SHAPE 128³ and DETAIL match within 1e-9 (the brief asks for 1e-3) on
    every axis, and the raw functions repeat at ±1, −3 and +7 tiles. Breaking the lattice wrap in a copy of the
    reference moves the faces 1.0 apart, so the test has teeth.
  - Range: every channel of a 14³ volume is in 0..1 and covers at least 0.4 between its 2nd and 98th percentiles.
  - Hash: offsets uniform, 0.05 is the ceiling on the correlation with a neighbouring cell (measured 0.005; a
    one-multiply hash measured 0.68 and was rejected).
  - Recipes keep to what the GLSL packs; the GLSL calls each octave with its recipe's weight, frequency and seed.
  - BLUE: seeded, each value 16 times, almost no power at the lowest frequencies, changes faster between neighbours
    than white noise, and the seam behaves like the interior.
- Dev server, then:
  - `http://localhost:<port>/lab/clouds?debug=noise`: the viewer. `&tier=medium` or `&tier=low` for the 64³ SHAPE.
  - The slider takes the pointer and the keyboard (Home, End, arrows; step 0.001), and has the label "Slice z".
  - The console prints one line per load, e.g. `[vol] noise high: SHAPE 128³ 49.8 ms, DETAIL 5.5 ms, BLUE 9.6 ms,
    shaders 25.5 ms → 92.0 ms (budget 150 ms)`, and warns when the total is over. `window.__vol.noiseMs` is the total.
  - In dev, StrictMode starts the renderer twice: two `[vol] tier` lines, one `[vol] noise` line (the runs are shared).
- `.shots/volumetric-lab/T1-noise.png`: the viewer at 1440×900, taken with
  `pnpm exec playwright screenshot --viewport-size=1440,900 --wait-for-timeout=4000 "<url>" <file>`.
- GPU against CPU: my readback scripts are scratch (not committed; see Requests for M1). The method: read
  `TEXTURE_BINDING_3D` on units 0 and 1 and `TEXTURE_BINDING_2D` on unit 2 between frames, attach each layer to a
  framebuffer, `readPixels`, and compare with `shapeAt` / `detailAt` at `(x + 0.5) / n`.

## Decisions (for M1/T8 to carry into docs/DECISIONS.md)

1. **Recipes.** SHAPE.R: Perlin fbm from 4, remapped by the inverted Worley at 4. SHAPE.G, B, A: Worley fbm from 2,
   4, 8. DETAIL.R, G, B: Worley fbm from 2, 4, 8. An fbm is three octaves (×1, ×2, ×4 at ⅝, ¼, ⅛).
2. **4 texels per Worley cell** is the resolution floor (`maxFrequency = size / 4`), so no octave goes past 16 at 64³
   and 8 at 32³. 64³ and 128³ are therefore the same noise, except that 128³ has one more octave in SHAPE's R (Perlin
   at 32) and A (Worley at 32). Tiers were kept alike on purpose: the BRIEF's checklist is judged on every tier.
3. **Contrast.** The Perlin fbm is a bell only 0.17 wide either side of 0, so R's Perlin is `clamp(0.5 + fbm / 0.6)`
   (`PERLIN_SPAN`), which leaves about 4% at 0 and 1% at 1. A Worley fbm channel is `clamp(0.5 + (fbm − 0.5) × 2)`
   (`WORLEY_GAIN`); without it the channels would use only 0.15..0.85 of the 8 bits.
4. **R's remap is the eroding one**, `remap(perlin, 1 − worley, 1, 0, 1)` clamped (the denominator floored at 1e-4).
   It was chosen against four other readings of "remapped by inverted Worley" on 2-D slices: it is the only one that
   gives the classic Perlin-Worley (puffs on clear air); the additive ones are washed-out grey. The cost is that 52%
   of R is exactly 0.
5. **Hash**: the cell (below 32 on every axis) and its seed (below 2^17) packed into one word, then MurmurHash3's
   finaliser. Two integer multiplies: with five, ten Worley evaluations took 22.7 ms against 15.6 ms.
6. **No integer remainder in the GLSL.** Every frequency is a power of two, so the lattice wraps with `& (freq − 1)`;
   the gradient index comes from `(h & 255) × 12 >> 8`. `% freq` made the shader 2.6× slower (GPUs have no divider).
7. **The GLSL is written out straight from the recipes**, not looped over a uniform table. Measured at 128³: the
   texture took 37 ms as a loop over a uniform table and 22 ms written out (the hash changed in between as well).
   Worley's loops keep constant bounds so the compiler unrolls them; bounds it cannot unroll took 41 ms.
8. **`genMs` is measured to completion with a 1×1 `readPixels`.** On Chrome through ANGLE Metal `gl.finish()` returns
   before the GPU is done (0.1 ms for a volume that took 20 ms); `readPixels` must wait. The total includes linking
   the shaders and the blue noise on the CPU.
9. **Mip chains are generated** (about 1 ms at 128³). `MIN_FILTER` stays LINEAR, so `texture()` reads level 0; the
   levels are there for `textureLod` on far steps and the light march. A periodic texture's mips are periodic.
10. **One run per context and tier.** Calls that overlap on one context share it (dev's StrictMode); a handle's
    `dispose()` frees the textures only when no other handle owns the run. The run is a later task, so a tier change
    asked for during a frame doesn't block that frame.
11. **Blue noise on the CPU**: void-and-cluster (Ulichney 1993) on a torus, a Gaussian of σ 1.5 cut at radius 5, in
    1/256 steps so the energies are integers in buckets. 10–12 ms once, memoised. Lowest spectral ring 4% of white
    noise; the seam's differences are 1.06× the interior's.
12. **Third-party code**: none copied. Perlin's noise, Worley's noise and void-and-cluster were implemented from their
    published descriptions. The hash finaliser is MurmurHash3's (public domain). An earlier draft used the constants
    0x7feb352d / 0x846ca68b, which come from a Stack Exchange snippet (CC BY-SA); it was replaced for this reason.
13. **The viewer** uses tokens only. Its backdrop is read from `--ink` at mount (the GLSL gets it as a uniform); the
    fallback for a canvas that cannot read the token is that colour's current value as floats. The slider's accent is
    `--text`, not `--signal`.

## Known issues

- **A browser's first load is over the budget.** The first shader a browser process compiles costs 160–900 ms here
  (the spread tracks the machine's load), and `genMs` includes it. Chrome and the OS cache the result; every later
  load was 44–92 ms. Restructuring the shader did not move this: within one process, later compiles of renamed shaders
  took 4–50 ms, so it is the compiler's warm-up, not this shader. The log line shows it as "shaders" plus the first
  draw. See Requests for M1.
- **The timings are from a loaded machine** (load average 20–40, other sessions running). Warm figures were stable
  (SHAPE 128³ 20–23 ms in isolation); cold ones varied 3–6×.
- **One GPU and driver only**: Apple M2, ANGLE Metal, headless Chromium. The GLSL keeps to GLSL ES 3.00 rules and
  avoids the known hazards (negative int→uint conversion, integer remainder), but it has not run on other hardware.
- **SHAPE.G is very low frequency** (two cells a tile): on a slice it reads as a few large sectors with straight
  creases. That is the Worley at 2, not an artefact: the CPU render of the same slice is identical.
- **The viewer needs the overlay** for its slider and captions (the renderer passes it on the lab page). Without one,
  the tiles still draw, at z = 0.5.
- **By-hand dev server**: the BRIEF's `NEXT_DIST_DIR=.next-dev` breaks `pnpm lint` (12,447 errors from generated
  files) and gets `tsconfig.json` auto-edited. I ran my server from `.scratch/next-dev` instead and reverted
  `tsconfig.json` before committing. See Requests for M1.
- **No independent review.** The code-reviewer and security-reviewer agents could not start (`No reasoning level
  selected`). I reviewed against the same checklist myself; three findings from it are fixed (a negative `int` →
  `uint` conversion in the Worley wrap, duplicate element ids under StrictMode, an overstated number in a comment).

## Requests for M1 (interface changes, with the reason)

1. **The budget on a browser's first load.** Either exempt the one-time shader compile from the 150 ms, or have
   `createNoiseTextures` link through `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` so the wait is off the main thread. It would
   overlap SHAPE's and DETAIL's compiles; it would not remove them.
2. **`.next-dev`.** Add `.next-dev/**` to the ESLint ignores and `.next-dev/` to `.gitignore`, or change the BRIEF §9
   by-hand command to a directory that is already ignored. Next also adds the directory's type paths to `tsconfig.json`
   on every start, which is a file no task owns.
3. **A committed GPU-against-CPU probe.** `scripts/probe/lab-noise.mts` is T6's to write. Until then the comparison
   in this hand-off cannot be rerun from the repo. My scratch scripts (`.scratch/t1/bench.mts`, `probe.mts`) have the
   method and I can hand them over; the essential part is about 60 lines.
4. **Document the mip chains in `types.ts`** ("mipmaps allowed" could say they exist), if T4 or T7 are to use
   `textureLod`.

## Notes for the next task

**T4 (density, march, lighting).**

- Sample: `texture(uShape, p).r` is Perlin-Worley, `.g`, `.b`, `.a` the Worley fbm at 2, 4 and 8 cells a tile;
  `texture(uDetail, p).rgb` the Worley fbm at 2, 4 and 8. `p` is any point of the tile, in units of the tile; wrap and
  REPEAT are set, so `p + windOffset` needs no `fract`. For the detail's slow evolution, any offset that drifts with
  `uTime` is valid.
- Scale is yours. The shape's billows are a quarter of its tile (Perlin at 4), its largest Worley cells half a tile.
- The channels are not centred the same way. R is 0 in 52% of the volume, with the median at 0 and the dense cores
  at 1 (24³ volume, 128³): above 0.5 in 19% of it, above 0.62 (what a coverage of 0.38 asks for) in 14%. So a
  coverage remap on R alone leaves little. A 2-D look suggested taking R as it comes, or under a mild height-gradient
  remap, and letting the Worley fbm channels erode it; the coverage belongs with the weather map. The G, B, A and
  DETAIL channels have their median at 0.45 and thin tails at both ends.
- For far steps and the light march, `textureLod(uShape, p, lod)` gives the box-filtered levels (level 1 is the 2×2×2
  mean); `lod` is clamped to the chain's 8 levels at 128³, 7 at 64³, 6 at 32³.
- A tier change replaces the `WebGLTexture` objects. `renderer.apply()` rebinds them every frame, so nothing to do.
- `?debug=noise` shows every channel as a slice at any z, to compare against what you see in the march.

**T7 (performance).** The textures take 9.6 MB at 128³ with their mips (1.2 MB at 64³, 0.15 MB for DETAIL) and 4 KB
for BLUE. Generation blocks the main thread for its duration, in a task of its own.

**T8 (tuning).** The two constants that shape the look are in `recipes.ts`: `PERLIN_SPAN` (0.6; lower is more
contrasty, with flat white cores below 0.5) and `WORLEY_GAIN` (2).
