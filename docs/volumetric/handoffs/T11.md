## What I built

1. **The resolve (`reprojectResolve`), rebuilt.** It has three estimates:
   - **spatial:** the pixel's own fresh sample. Without one, it's the bilinear mix of the 2 or 4 fresh samples around
     the pixel, and each cloudy sample is also weighted by depth (relative to the group's opacity-weighted
     `gCloudDist`; σ = 25%).
   - **box:** min/max in YCoCg plus transmittance. A fresh pixel uses the 3×3 around it. A non-fresh pixel uses the
     samples its spatial estimate mixes, padded by 0.5 × the 3×3 range (`REPROJECT_BOX_PAD`).
   - **history:** sampled with a 5-tap Catmull-Rom (`reprojectHistory`), then clipped toward the box centre. Each
     extent has a floor: 3% of the box's brightest Y in Y, Co and Cg, and 0.01 in T.
   - out = mix(spatial, clipped history, w), with w = clamp(1 − off/τ), τ = 0.2. w is 0 when the history is invalid
     or off-screen. A fresh pixel caps w at 1 − α, with α 0.25 and the 1, ½, ⅓ reset kept. D5 is untouched.
2. **Layered output (`out=layered`).**
   - **The background layer** (`createBackgroundLayer`) is its own WebGL2 context at the full drawing buffer. It
     runs `displayColor(background(viewRay))`, with its own blue-noise texture and timer query. It draws on a reset,
     and on the frame *after* every 4th march, so no frame pays for a march and a full-resolution background. On
     every other frame it draws nothing.
   - **The cloud canvas** is `{alpha: true, premultipliedAlpha: true}`, with its drawing buffer at s_out × the full
     size. `reprojectLayered` gives A = 1 − T and C = D(c + T·b) − T·D(b), where b is a background cache at s_out
     inside the cloud context, refreshed on the same frame as the layer.
   - **The alpha is lifted by default.** It is raised to the least value that keeps every channel C ≤ A, which is
     still exact for that b. `lift=0` gives the brief's clamp, and `clampviz=1` paints where that clamp binds.
3. **`hold=1`** (T7's option 1, in both modes): draw only on march frames, resets and background refreshes.
4. **Wiring.**
   - `renderer.ts`: the output mode, the context attributes, the background layer, `__vol.out`, `__vol.bgGpuMs` and
     `__vol.bgDrawMs`. `applyReviewParams` is not touched (it doesn't exist on this base).
   - `LabClient.tsx`: the background canvas, always in the page. It only gets a context with `out=layered`.
   - `CloudPipeline.invalidate()` in `types.ts` (T7's Request 1).

## Checkerboard: what the lattice was

The brief blamed the 3×3 clamp. Measured, that's not the main cause.

1. **T9's lattice metric can't separate the defect from cloud detail on A.**
   - The method: a reference frame, frozen A marched at scale 1 per pixel. It reads 0.006–0.0098 per crop, about
     T8-r3's own 0.0054–0.0085.
   - The metric is [[1,−1],[−1,1]] at the texel grid, and real edge and detail energy fills it.
   - So "≤ 30% of T8-r3" is unreachable without blurring below the reference.
   - I added two metrics:
     - **class asymmetry:** per-Bayer-class high-pass RMS on the phase-locked texel grid. The reference reads 1.5%.
     - **residual:** against the reference, frozen. The reference against itself reads 3.9 ×10⁻³.
2. **Under motion, the lattice is reprojection lag.**
   - Frozen T7 shows no lattice (class asymmetry 4.7%). Moving T7 shows it (14.8%, mean of 5 captures).
   - Each march, the wind moves the field a fraction of a texel. A small velocity or depth error then lags the
     non-fresh classes, and the running mean grows that lag about 4/α times.
   - Bilinear history blurs it into "cotton wool", which is part of T8's soft edges. Catmull-Rom shows it as stripes
     across the wind (`velocity-scale-test.png`: 1.25 × the modelled velocity removes them, 0.8 × doubles them).
   - I found no error in the wind, drift, timing or depth model, and I didn't add a fudge factor.
   - The 3×3 box of samples 2 texels apart is too loose to see sub-texel lag. A box of the nearest samples is not.
3. **Two things in the brief's own design hurt.** Both are measured and fixed.
   - **The γ = 1 variance box is worse than min/max:** class asymmetry 16% against 6% (3 captures each). One cloudy
     sample among nine clear ones (a wisp) lies about 2.7σ from μ, so the box clips the wisp out of its own history.
   - **A 4-D clip with no extent floor collapses on grey cloud.** Co and Cg are about 0 in all nine samples, so the
     history's fp16 chroma noise clipped every channel to the centre, and w was about 0 on almost every cloud pixel
     (seen in a w view).

| A at 1440×900 DPR 2 | lattice (T9) | class asymmetry | hp-rms (ref 14.7) | frozen residual (floor 3.9) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T8-r3 (moving, 3 shots) | 0.0068 | 21.4% | 11.5 | – |
| T7 resolve, this branch (moving, 5) | 0.0070 | 14.8% | 9.0 | 5.5 |
| Brief as written (γ 1 variance, bilinear) | 0.0083 | 43% (1 shot) | 11.5 | 8.1 |
| **T11 final (moving, 5)** | **0.0104** | **10.6%** | 15.7 | 9.1 |
| Reference (scale 1, frozen) | 0.0083 | 1.5% | 14.7 | 3.9 |

Visually (`lattice-and-drift.png`), T8-r3's dot lattice in the wisps is gone, and the wisps read as irregular detail.
The drift pair shows no ghosting. Residuals:

- Edges carry a fine grain, a little more high-pass energy than the reference (15.7 against 14.7).
- Faint streaks across the wind remain on some cloud bodies (the leftover lag).
- T9's lattice number is *higher*, because the history is sharper. That metric reads sharpness as lattice.

## Layered output: decision table

Desktop is 1920×1080 DPR 2 on the high tier. Mobile is 375×812 DPR 3 on the low tier.

- **GPU** is the mean ms per frame, amortised over A, B and C, on ANGLE Metal on an M2. Runs vary by about ±0.5 ms.
  The cloud and background contexts are timed separately, and "sum" is their total.
- **Compositor** is the Chrome trace's VizCompositorThread ms per frame, for A.
- **ΔL** is the luma difference from single over the same frozen frame, box-filtered over 8×8 device px. It is given
  for A, the worst state, as mean / p99, at 1440 and then at 375.
- **The noise floor** (single against single) is **0.31 / 6.35** at 1440 and **0.43 / 8.73** at 375.
- **Clamp-bound** is the share of cloud pixels where the brief's C ≤ A clamp moves a value by more than one 8-bit
  level (A / B / C, at 1440). With the alpha lifted it is 0 by construction.

- **B and C** are well inside the tolerance in every lifted mode:
  - B: at most 0.14% mean and 2.5% p99 at 1440, and at most 0.19% and 4.0% at 375.
  - C: at most 0.07% and 0.8%.
- **No seam or halo** at 100% along edges or around B's sun (`layered-A-edge.png`, `layered-B-sun.png`).
  - At 0.35, B's small bright fragments are slightly blockier than single, because the browser's bilinear stretch
    replaces the full-resolution bilateral upsample.
  - At 0.5 they can't be told apart.
- **The brief's clamp** binds on A's thin cloud over blue sky and on B's silver-lit fragments, by more than 8 levels
  in most of those pixels, and it greys the silver lining.
- **The extra layer** costs the compositor +0.24 ms per frame at 0.35 and +0.03 ms at 0.5 (desktop), and about 0 on
  mobile.
- **Frame time.** With tracing on, every mode held 60 fps, with no rAF interval over 25 ms except 0.6% with
  layered + hold on desktop.
  - The per-frame GPU timer reads 11–15 ms at p95 on desktop in *every* mode, single included. Splitting the march
    across two frames didn't lower it and added +0.5–1 ms amortised, so I reverted the split.
  - The cause is the M2's GPU clock dropping between bursts (T7 saw the same). This machine can't judge the "≤ 8 ms
    per frame" rule with timer queries. It needs a device with fixed clocks, or the trace.
- **hold's drift step** (part 3, A, from the scene camera and the wind model):
  - 3.25 device px per 15 Hz step at the top of the view (50°, 1920×1080 DPR 2).
  - 2.71 at 1440×900 DPR 2, and 3.66 at 375×812 DPR 3.
  - 0.16 at the lowest cloud the march reaches (2°).
  - That's a visible judder.

**Targets.** Mobile low ≤ 3 ms: met in every mode. Desktop high ≤ 2.5 ms: **not met** by any mode. The closest are
layered 0.35 + hold at 2.7–2.9 and layered 0.35 at 3.3–3.6.

## Recommendation: the shipping architecture

**Layered, lifted alpha, s_out = the tier's scale (0.35), no hold.**

- It removes both full-resolution passes and cuts desktop GPU by about 30% (5.0 → 3.4 ms).
- It is indistinguishable from single within A's noise floor, and well inside tolerance on B and C.
- It matches how the home page already composes fixed layers.
- Use s_out 0.5 if the owner sees the 0.35 blockiness on bright fragments near a low sun. It costs about +0.3 ms.
- Not hold: −0.6 ms for a 3 px judder at the top of the view.

**The desktop gap.** What remains is no longer the composite. It is the march and resolve, about 2.5 ms amortised
at 60 Hz on desktop high (Catmull-Rom and the larger resolve add roughly 0.3–0.5 ms over T7's). Closing it means
fewer rays or steps on desktop high: a 4×4 pattern at 60 Hz, or 48 steps. Or the owner re-baselines the desktop
target at about 3.5 ms.

## Decisions (logged in `docs/DECISIONS.md` #205–#210; M2 renumbered them, T10 had taken #198–#204)

- **D1:** the resolve takes the brief's structure, but its box is min/max (not μ ± γσ), tight for non-fresh pixels,
  with an extent floor and Catmull-Rom history.
- **D2:** T9's lattice metric isn't a pass gate for A; class asymmetry and the residual against a reference are.
- **D3:** layered alpha is lifted, not clamped.
- **D4:** the background layer refreshes on the frame after the march.
- **D5:** hold stays in the lab, not the recommendation.
- **D6:** per-frame GPU peaks on the M2 aren't a valid "8 ms frame" test.
